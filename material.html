How the list is figured

Precut studs: 2x4 and 2x6 studs, kings, jacks and backers cut at exactly 92 5/8".

Studs and Trimmers: the same parts at other lengths. Under 92 5/8" are cut from 92 5/8" stock (two per stud when 46 5/16" or less), up to 104 5/8" from 104 5/8", up to 10' from 10'. Anything longer goes to lineal.

Lineal: plates, sills, headers, cripples, blocking and other 2x4/2x6 parts, nested into 12' boards. Pieces over 12' get their own 14', 16' or longer board. Treated (PT) and SYP plates are listed on their own.

Headers: 2x8, 2x10 and 2x12 headers of each size all come out of one board length between 8' and 16' (2' steps). Every length that fits the longest header is tried and the one with the least total scrap is used. A header longer than 16' gets its own board in the next even length and nothing else is cut from it. 1x lumber follows the same rule. 2x4 and 2x6 headers are in the lineal. LVL and other engineered pieces are listed by the next even length.

Paneling: number of walls divided by 5, rounded up, in 12' boards. That count is used for both the 2x6 stack blocking and the 2x4 stack bracing.

Sheet size: picked from the standard wall height. Up to 97" uses 4x8 sheets, 97" to 109" uses 4x9, and 109" to 121" uses 4x10. The same size is used for shear walls.

Wall sheets: take every wall that has sheathing on it, add up the length of their bottom plates, divide by 4' per sheet, and round up to the nearest 5.

Shear wall sheets: for each wall marked as a shear wall, its bottom plate length (one side) divided by 4' per sheet, rounded up to the nearest 5. Shear walls are only ever sheathed on one side. An exterior wall marked as a shear wall adds nothing, since its sheathing is already in the wall sheets above.

Tall walls: exterior and shear walls taller than the standard wall height get extra sheathing. Up to 2' over standard adds 25%, 2' to 4' over adds 50%, and more than 4' over adds 100%. Standard height is the most common panel height unless you set it under Allowances.
